Six questions to ask of a sentence

FunctionAsk yourself
AnswerWhich exact part of the prompt does this sentence answer?
DevelopDoes it explain how or why the claim works?
SupportDoes the example demonstrate the preceding reason?
QualifyIs the group, condition or certainty stated accurately?
ConnectCan the reader identify the relationship and any reference?

Four upgrades you can practise now

These examples improve specificity and reasoning. They illustrate writing choices, not an automatic band increase.

From a label to an explained mechanism

Less developed

Public transport is good. It reduces traffic. It is very important for society.

More specific

Reliable public transport can reduce congestion by giving commuters an alternative to driving. If buses connect residential areas with workplaces and run frequently, a car becomes less necessary for daily travel. For example, a direct peak-hour route may allow residents along it to leave their cars at home. Fewer separate car journeys would then place less pressure on the same roads.

What changed
  • Reliable services and commuters define the conditions and group.
  • The if-clause explains why behaviour changes.
  • The example illustrates the same mechanism rather than adding an unrelated benefit.
Source basis: British Council paragraph development ↗

From listing to weighing

Less developed

Tourism has two advantages and one disadvantage, so the advantages outweigh the disadvantages.

More specific

Tourism can put pressure on popular sites, especially during busy seasons. Nevertheless, employment in accommodation, food services and transport can benefit households across the local economy. Where visitor limits reduce crowding without removing this demand, the economic benefits may carry greater weight. The judgement therefore depends on both the reach of the benefits and the destination's ability to manage the costs.

What changed
  • Both sides receive a real effect.
  • Reach and manageability provide a reason for the judgement.
  • The condition is explained in the paragraph rather than added only at the end.
Source basis: Understanding Task 2 prompts ↗

From connector stacking to clear reference

Less developed

Firstly, city rent is expensive. Moreover, city rent is a problem. Furthermore, people cannot save money.

More specific

High rents can absorb a large share of a worker's income. This housing cost leaves less money for savings and other essential purchases. The pressure is particularly severe for lower-income residents, whose earnings may not rise as quickly as their living expenses.

What changed
  • This housing cost clearly refers to the preceding idea.
  • Each sentence extends the previous one.
  • The named group makes the claim more precise.
Source basis: British Council paragraph development ↗

From an introduction to an overview

Less developed

The chart shows the percentages of workers using different types of transport.

More specific

Overall, cars were the most common option among workers aged 30 and above, whereas the youngest group was more likely to use buses. Cycling was also considerably more common among younger workers.

What changed
  • The first sentence is useful as an introduction, but does not summarise a pattern.
  • The improved overview identifies the main ranking and a second contrast.
  • The details can now support these selected features with percentages.

If you get stuck, take a specific next step

The moves below are rehearsal advice. Adapt them to the actual task and protect time for both responses.

01 · No ideas

Name one relevant group, one change in its behaviour and one practical consequence. Keep two ideas you can explain.

For a remote-work question: commuters save travel time; new staff may receive less immediate guidance.

Avoid: Do not fill the answer with a memorised essay on technology.

Source basis: IDP brainstorming ideas ↗
02 · An unfamiliar word

Use a familiar description that preserves meaning, or keep an accurate topic keyword. Change sentence structure if a synonym is uncertain.

Use people who travel to work instead of an unfamiliar synonym for commuters.

Avoid: Do not replace a term with a word whose meaning or grammar you do not know.

Source basis: IDP paraphrasing in Tasks 1 and 2 ↗
03 · A sentence is getting too long

Complete the main claim, add a full stop, then explain it in the next sentence. Keep one controlled subordinate clause.

The service is unreliable. Because buses are often late, commuters may choose to drive.

Avoid: Do not force several clauses together to display complexity.

Source basis: British Council grammar guidance ↗
04 · The question does not fit a category

Underline the actual questions and assign each required answer a place in the plan.

Why + effects on individuals and society means a cause and two sets of effects, not a solution paragraph.

Avoid: Do not select a familiar template before checking the instructions.

Source basis: Understanding Task 2 prompts ↗
05 · The word count is short

Extend a relevant explanation: how does the effect occur, who is affected, and what situation illustrates it?

After claiming that a loan is risky, explain interest, repayment conditions and the effect on a new worker's budget.

Avoid: Do not add an unrelated introduction, repeated conclusion or invented statistic.

Source basis: British Council Academic Writing preparation ↗
06 · Only a few minutes remain

Check for a missing required part first. Add a concise relevant overview or judgement, then correct errors that obscure meaning.

If the second named group is missing, add its specific effect before polishing a sophisticated word.

Avoid: Do not abandon one whole task to perfect the other.

Source basis: IELTS Academic Writing format ↗
07 · Task 1 looks crowded

Mark the main extremes, shared pattern and exception; make two meaningful groups before reporting selected details.

Describe a group of rising series together, then the declining exception with supporting values.

Avoid: Do not narrate every data point in visual order.

08 · You have changed your opinion

Choose the position you can support and make the introduction, body and conclusion agree with it.

If your body supports a limited policy, state the same limitation in the opening and ending.

Avoid: Do not leave contradictory verdicts in different paragraphs.

01Agree or disagreeState and support your degree of agreement
Recognise the instruction

To what extent do you agree or disagree? / Do you agree or disagree?

Give a clear degree of agreement. A fully supportive position can be developed through two reasons; a fully opposing position can use two objections.
Original practice prompt
Schools should teach students how to manage money. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

A workable plan

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the claim and state your position
  2. Body 1: first reason, explanation and a specific illustration
  3. Body 2: second reason, explanation and relevant support
  4. Conclusion: restate the position and its main basis

A flexible frame

Replace the brackets with a real answer. The frame is a starting structure; develop explanations and support to produce a complete response.

I agree that [specific claim], because [reason 1] and [reason 2].

One reason is that [complete claim]. This is because [mechanism]. For example, [specific situation and its result]. This shows how [link back to the claim].

A further reason is that [complete claim]. When [condition], [consequence]. For instance, [relevant illustration]. This can help [group] to [specific outcome].

In conclusion, I support [position], mainly because [brief summary of the reasons].
A completed response sentence

I support teaching financial skills at school because students need to manage everyday spending and assess the risks of borrowing.

Check this: A general discussion of why money matters does not explain why schools should teach financial skills. Respond to limits such as all, only or the best.

02Partial agreementDefine the limits of your position
Recognise the instruction

To what extent… / all / only / the best / rather than

Specify where the claim holds and where it does not. Partial agreement is a position within an opinion task, rather than a separate official question type.
Original practice prompt
Working from home is the best option for all employees. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

A workable plan

  1. Introduction: state the situations you accept and reject
  2. Body 1: the case where the approach works
  3. Body 2: an important exception and why it matters
  4. Conclusion: keep the same limits

A flexible frame

Replace the brackets with a real answer. The frame is a starting structure; develop explanations and support to produce a complete response.

Although [approach] is useful for [group A], I do not consider it the best option for [group B].

For [group A], [approach] can [benefit]. This is because [reason]. For example, [situation and result].

However, [group B] often require [different condition]. If [approach is applied without this condition], [consequence]. For instance, [specific illustration].

In conclusion, [approach] is suitable for [scope], but its value depends on [relevant condition].
A completed response sentence

Working from home can suit independent roles, but it is less practical for employees who need specialist equipment or direct contact with customers.

Check this: Do not begin with partial agreement and finish with complete agreement. A rather than comparison needs a reason for choosing or combining the alternatives.

Recognise the instruction

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Explain the reasoning behind view A and view B, then give your own judgement. Describing a view does not mean you personally accept it.
Original practice prompt
Some people think university courses should focus on job skills. Others believe universities should provide a broad education. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

A workable plan

  1. Introduction: identify the disagreement and your position
  2. Body 1: explain the first view and its supporting reason
  3. Body 2: explain the second view and evaluate it
  4. Conclusion: make your judgement clear

04Advantages and disadvantagesDevelop both sides of the change
Recognise the instruction

What are the advantages and disadvantages of…?

Discuss meaningful benefits and drawbacks. A comparative verdict is unnecessary unless the question asks for one.
Original practice prompt
More people are choosing to live in large cities. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this trend?

A workable plan

  1. Introduction: identify the trend and its two main effects
  2. Body 1: benefit and how it arises
  3. Body 2: drawback and how it arises
  4. Conclusion: summarise both sides

A flexible frame

Replace the brackets with a real answer. The frame is a starting structure; develop explanations and support to produce a complete response.

[Paraphrase the trend]. It can improve [benefit], but may also create difficulties related to [drawback].

One advantage is that [claim]. This allows [group] to [outcome]. For example, [specific situation].

A major drawback is that [claim]. This is because [mechanism]. For instance, [situation and consequence].

In conclusion, [trend] offers [benefit], while also bringing challenges involving [drawback].
A completed response sentence

Large cities offer access to more jobs and services, but high housing costs can place considerable pressure on household budgets.

Check this: Jobs, transport and pollution are labels, not developed arguments. Explain how access to several employers improves a job seeker's options.

05Weigh advantages against disadvantagesExplain why one side matters more
Recognise the instruction

Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? / Do the disadvantages outweigh the advantages?

Discuss both sides and give a comparative judgement. Compare significance, reach or duration; counting points alone does not show why one side is stronger.
Original practice prompt
International tourism is becoming more common. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?

A workable plan

  1. Introduction: state which side carries greater weight
  2. Body 1: the weaker side and its real consequences
  3. Body 2: the stronger side and the basis of your comparison
  4. Conclusion: confirm the judgement and any necessary condition

A flexible frame

Replace the brackets with a real answer. The frame is a starting structure; develop explanations and support to produce a complete response.

Although [trend] can cause [drawback], I believe its benefits are greater because [comparative reason].

A significant concern is that [drawback and mechanism]. For example, [specific consequence]. This matters because [effect on a group].

However, [benefit] can have a greater impact. [Explain and illustrate]. This is more significant because [scope, duration or severity].

In conclusion, the benefits outweigh the drawbacks, provided that [condition supported in the essay].
A completed response sentence

Tourism can strain popular sites, but its support for local employment can be more significant if visitor numbers and environmental pressures are managed responsibly.

Check this: Check which side the question puts first. A concluding outweigh statement cannot compensate for discussing only one side.

06Positive or negative developmentEvaluate the overall direction of change
Recognise the instruction

Is this a positive or negative development?

Judge the overall change and support that judgement. You can develop two supporting effects or acknowledge a drawback before explaining why your overall judgement remains valid.
Original practice prompt
More employees can now work from home. Is this a positive or negative development?

A workable plan

  1. Introduction: state the overall evaluation
  2. Body 1: main effect and why it matters
  3. Body 2: another effect; explain a relevant limit if needed
  4. Conclusion: keep the same evaluation

A flexible frame

Replace the brackets with a real answer. The frame is a starting structure; develop explanations and support to produce a complete response.

[Paraphrase the change]. I consider it largely positive because [main effects].

One benefit is that [claim]. This means [mechanism or consequence]. For example, [specific illustration].

Another benefit is [claim]. Although [relevant limitation], [explain why it does not reverse the overall judgement].

In conclusion, I view this development positively overall, as [main basis], despite [limit].
A completed response sentence

The growth of remote work is largely positive because it reduces commuting and makes suitable jobs accessible to people living outside major business centres.

Check this: Evaluate the actual development. Do not replace the question with a generic argument about whether technology is good.

Recognise the instruction

What causes…? What problems…? What measures or solutions…?

Separate causes, problems, effects and solutions. For causes plus solutions, explain what creates the issue and how a measure addresses that cause.
Original practice prompt
Traffic congestion is a serious problem in many cities. What are the causes of this problem, and what measures can be taken to solve it?

A workable plan

  1. Introduction: identify the main cause and a suitable response
  2. Body 1: cause and the mechanism behind it
  3. Body 2: actor, action and how the action would work
  4. Conclusion: summarise the cause-to-solution link

A flexible frame

Replace the brackets with a real answer. The frame is a starting structure; develop explanations and support to produce a complete response.

[Paraphrase the issue]. It is partly caused by [cause], and could be reduced through [measure].

One major cause is [cause]. When [situation], [explain how the problem arises]. For example, [illustration].

To address this, [actor] should [specific action]. This would [change the cause] by [mechanism]. For instance, [practical implementation].

In conclusion, [problem] is linked to [cause], and [matched measure] could help reduce it.
A completed response sentence

Unreliable public transport encourages car use. More frequent services connecting residential areas with workplaces would give commuters a practical alternative.

Check this: Raise awareness is too vague unless you explain whose behaviour would change and how. If asked about problems, describe consequences instead of substituting causes.

Why…? What effects…? / Why…? Do you think…? / What problems…? Do they outweigh…?

02Does remote work suit everyone?Task 2 · Partial agreement · 268 words · original practice
Working from home is the best option for all employees. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Working from home has become a practical option for many employees. Although it can be beneficial for people who carry out independent tasks, I do not agree that it is the best arrangement for all workers.

For employees whose work mainly involves reading, writing or analysing information, working at home can offer clear benefits. They may be able to concentrate in a quiet room and organise their day around demanding tasks. They also avoid travelling to an office, which can save both time and money. For example, a writer who has agreed deadlines may be able to complete an article effectively without being in the same building as a manager. If communication and expectations are clear, the location of this person's work may have little effect on its quality. In such situations, remote work can be a sensible choice.

However, other jobs depend on equipment, direct contact or close cooperation. A laboratory employee needs access to specialist tools, while a shop assistant needs to serve customers in person. Even in office jobs, new employees may benefit from observing experienced colleagues and asking questions immediately. If everyone works alone at home, these opportunities can become harder to arrange. Regular video meetings may help, but they cannot provide every kind of practical support. Employers therefore need to consider the actual duties of a role rather than assume that one arrangement will suit the whole organisation.

In conclusion, working from home can be effective for employees who complete independent tasks, but it is less suitable for roles requiring equipment or direct interaction. The best arrangement should depend on the work involved.

04Benefits and costs of city lifeTask 2 · Advantages and disadvantages · 266 words · original practice
More people are choosing to live in large cities. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this trend?

An increasing number of people choose to make their homes in large cities. This trend can provide access to employment and useful services, but it can also create difficulties related to living costs and daily travel.

One main advantage of city life is the range of opportunities available. Large cities often contain many employers in different industries, so residents can look for work that matches their skills. If they lose one job, they may also have more alternatives nearby. In addition, universities, hospitals and other services are often easier to reach than in a small town. For example, someone who needs specialist medical care may be able to attend appointments without travelling to another region. These opportunities can be particularly valuable for young adults who are starting their careers and want to continue their education while working.

However, living in a large city can place pressure on household budgets. Strong demand for housing may lead to high rents, leaving residents with less money for food, transport and savings. In conclusion, large cities can offer valuable employment, education and healthcare opportunities. At the same time, high housing costs and demanding journeys can make everyday life difficult for some residents.

05Does tourism bring greater benefits?Task 2 · Outweigh · 269 words · original practice
International tourism is becoming more common. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?

International travel has become more common, allowing many destinations to receive visitors from abroad. Although tourism can create pressure on local communities, I believe its advantages are greater when destinations manage visitor numbers responsibly.

A major disadvantage is that too many tourists can make daily life difficult for residents. Popular streets may become crowded, and demand for short stays can reduce the supply of housing for local people. Natural sites can also suffer when visitors leave waste or walk through fragile areas. For example, a small coastal town may struggle to collect rubbish during a busy holiday season. These problems are serious because they affect the people who live there throughout the year, rather than only those visiting for a few days. Tourism therefore needs clear rules and adequate local services.

However, visitors can support employment and businesses that might otherwise have limited customers. Hotels, restaurants and transport providers need workers, and small shops can earn income by selling local products. 18 errors worth checking first

01 Subject–verb agreement

Problematic form

Many students studies online.

Accurate alternative

Many students study online.

A plural subject takes study, not studies.

02 Uncountable nouns

Problematic form

These informations are useful.

Accurate alternative

This information is useful.

Information, advice and equipment are normally uncountable.

03 Articles

Problematic form

Government should build hospital.

Accurate alternative

The government should build a hospital.

A singular countable noun normally needs a determiner.

04 Sentence fragments

Problematic form

Because public transport is unreliable.

Accurate alternative

People drive because public transport is unreliable.

A because-clause normally needs a main clause.

05 Double conjunctions

Problematic form

Although it is expensive, but it is useful.

Accurate alternative

Although it is expensive, it is useful.

Do not combine although and but in this pattern.

06 Comma splices

Problematic form

It saves time, it reduces costs.

Accurate alternative

It saves time and reduces costs.

Join complete sentences with appropriate punctuation or a conjunction.

07 Access as a verb or noun

Problematic form

People can access to courses.

Accurate alternative

People can access courses. / People have access to courses.

The verb access does not take to; the noun does.

08 Allow + object + infinitive

Problematic form

It allows people working at home.

Accurate alternative

It allows people to work at home.

Use allow somebody to do something.

09 Thereby

Problematic form

It reduces costs, thereby people save money.

Accurate alternative

It reduces costs, thereby helping people save money.

Thereby is an adverb; it does not independently join two clauses.

10 Despite and although

Problematic form

Despite it is expensive, people use it.

Accurate alternative

Although it is expensive, people use it. / Despite its cost, people use it.

Despite takes a noun phrase or -ing form; although takes a clause.

11 With structures

Problematic form

With the figure was rising.

Accurate alternative

The total grew, with the figure rising to 50.

With + noun + -ing is a supplementary pattern, not its only possible structure.

12 Changes in percentages

Problematic form

It rose by 30% from 20% to 30%.

Accurate alternative

It rose by 10 percentage points, from 20% to 30%.

Distinguish the endpoint, absolute change and relative change.

13 Comparable objects

Problematic form

The number was higher than Germany.

Accurate alternative

The number was higher than that in Germany.

Compare a figure with another figure.

14 Tenses

Problematic form

In 2010, the share increases to 40%.

Accurate alternative

In 2010, the share increased to 40%.

Completed past dates usually call for past tense; mark forecasts appropriately.

15 Word forms

Problematic form

The figure rose significant.

Accurate alternative

The figure rose significantly. / There was a significant rise.

The verb takes an adverb; the noun can take an adjective.

16 Effect and affect

Problematic form

This has an effect to children.

Accurate alternative

This has an effect on children. / This affects children.

Effect is a noun; affect is a verb.

17 Unjustified certainty

Problematic form

Technology solves all problems.

Accurate alternative

Technology can help solve some problems.

Keep the strength of your wording consistent with your reasoning.

18 Unsupported causation

Problematic form

Both figures increased, so one caused the other.

Accurate alternative

Both figures increased over the period.

A graph showing two changes does not establish their cause.

Before the test, recall these six things

  1. What must a discuss, outweigh or multi-part response cover?
  2. What main feature would you put in a trend, static, map or process overview?
  3. How would you explain a claim with a mechanism and an illustration?
  4. How do from, to and by differ? What is a percentage point?
  5. Can you control a because, although or if sentence and a clear comparison?
  6. Can you finish within 20 / 40 minutes and check the response?
